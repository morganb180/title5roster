# Title 5 septic inspections at property transfer, explained

Title 5 of the Massachusetts State Environmental Code (310 CMR 15.000) governs septic systems. Most sales of a property with a septic system or cesspool need a **system inspection by a MassDEP-approved System Inspector**.

## What is required at transfer

* An inspection **within 2 years before** the sale. It stays valid **3 years** if the system was pumped every year and the report includes the records.
* If weather prevents inspection, it can happen **up to 6 months after** the transfer, if the seller notifies the buyer in writing.
* The inspector must use the MassDEP form and submit the report within **30 days**, usually to the local Board of Health. The buyer gets a copy.
* One inspection covers multiple sales within its 2-year window.

## Common exemptions

* Transfers between spouses, parents and children, or full siblings, and certain trusts.
* Refinancing, taking a mortgage, or changes where no new parties are introduced.
* A signed, recorded agreement with the Board of Health to upgrade or connect to sewer within 2 years.
* A new or upgraded system with a Certificate of Compliance in the past 2 years (up to 3 with pumping).

## Pass, conditional pass, fail

* **Pass:** the system can protect public health and the environment as of the inspection date. That is not a guarantee it will keep working.
* **Conditional pass:** specific components need repair or replacement, such as a metal or cracked tank, a broken pipe, an uneven distribution box, or a faulty pump chamber. Once repaired with Board of Health approval, the system passes. Soil absorption systems and cesspools cannot get a conditional pass.
* **Fail:** the system must be upgraded within 2 years unless the Board of Health or MassDEP sets another schedule. The obligation remains even if the owner cancels the sale.

## Condos, shared and large systems

Condominiums with 5 or more units inspect all systems every 3 years. Smaller condos either inspect every 3 years or inspect the unit's system within 2 years before its sale. Shared systems are inspected every 3 years.

## Complaints

Contact your regional MassDEP office's Title 5 staff. Suspected falsified reports: Massachusetts Environmental Strike Force, 617-556-1000 or 1-888-VIOLATE.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long is a Title 5 inspection good for?**

Generally 2 years for a sale. It stays valid for 3 years if the system was pumped at least once a year and the report includes the pumping records.

**Who pays for the Title 5 inspection?**

The property owner (seller) is responsible for arranging it. Buyer and seller can shift that responsibility in writing before the transfer, as long as the inspection still happens on time.

**Can a town health agent do my inspection?**

Only a MassDEP-approved System Inspector can do it, and an inspector may not act as an agent of the Board of Health while also representing the owner.

**What if it's winter and the ground is frozen?**

If weather prevents the inspection before the sale, it can be done up to 6 months after, provided the seller tells the buyer in writing.
