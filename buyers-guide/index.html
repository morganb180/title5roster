Buyer's guide: Title 5 septic inspections

  1. Ask for the report early. You are entitled to a copy. Check it's on the MassDEP form and signed by an approved inspector (verify on the NEIWPCC list or our town pages).
  2. Check the date. It must be within 2 years before closing, or 3 with annual pumping records.
  3. Read the result. A conditional pass lists components to fix. A failure means an upgrade within 2 years.
  4. Negotiate. Repair before closing, a price credit, or an escrow holdback are common. Get quotes before you agree on amounts.
  5. Understand the limits. A pass describes the system on inspection day. If you plan to add bedrooms, a new inspection and possibly an upgrade may be needed.
  6. Check the town. Some Boards of Health have stricter local rules.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy a house with a failed Title 5?

Yes. A failed system must be upgraded within 2 years (unless the Board of Health sets another schedule), and buyer and seller often negotiate a price credit or escrow. Talk to your attorney and lender.

Do I get a copy of the report?

Yes. The person acquiring title must receive a copy of the inspection report.
