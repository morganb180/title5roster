Seller's guide: Title 5 septic inspections

  1. Confirm you need one. Most sales do; see exemptions.
  2. Hire a MassDEP-approved System Inspector. Only approved inspectors' reports count. Find one by town.
  3. Inspect early. Inspecting before listing gives you time to handle a conditional pass or failure. The report is good for 2 years (3 with annual pumping).
  4. Gather records: pumping receipts, the system's as-built plan from the Board of Health, and past inspections.
  5. Plan for winter. If frozen ground prevents inspection, you can inspect up to 6 months after closing but must notify the buyer in writing.
  6. Give the buyer a copy. The inspector files the report with the Board of Health within 30 days.

Frequently asked questions

When should I schedule the inspection?

Before listing is safest. You then know the result before negotiating, and one report covers you for 2 years.

What if my system fails?

You must upgrade within 2 years even if you don't sell. Many sellers fix it before closing or negotiate a credit or escrow with the buyer.
