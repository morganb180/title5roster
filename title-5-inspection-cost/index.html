How much does a Title 5 inspection cost?

Last updated 2026-10-13. Checked against the official sources listed at the end of this page. Not legal advice: confirm local rules with the town Board of Health.

Reviewed by Title 5 Roster staff

Short answer: most Title 5 inspections cost about $300–$900 (often $400–$600). Pumping, if needed, is usually extra at roughly $250–$500. MassDEP doesn't set or regulate inspection fees, so prices vary by inspector and town (Mass.gov consumer tips).

What affects the price of a Title 5 inspection?

Pumping and locating or digging up components, the system type, the number of systems and bedrooms, travel and season, and any Board of Health filing fee.

  • Whether the tank must be pumped and the components located or dug up
  • System type (cesspool, conventional, innovative/alternative technology)
  • Number of systems and bedrooms
  • Travel and season (frozen ground)
  • Town filing fees, where the Board of Health charges one

Who pays for a Title 5 inspection?

Title 5 doesn't say. In practice the seller usually orders the inspection, and repair costs are often negotiated as a price credit or escrow.

Title 5 doesn't say who pays for the inspection or repairs. In practice the seller usually orders the inspection, and repair costs are often negotiated as a price credit or escrow (Mass.gov).

Ask each inspector for a written price that says whether pumping and excavation are included.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a Title 5 inspection cost?

Usually a few hundred dollars: 2026 consumer guides put most inspections at about $300–$900, with $400–$600 common. MassDEP does not regulate inspection fees, so get written prices and ask whether pumping is included.

Does the inspection price include pumping?

Often not. MassDEP's consumer tips say to ask, because pumping is frequently billed separately; guides put it at roughly $250–$500.

What does it cost if the system fails?

Replacement varies with soil, groundwater and lot. Recent Massachusetts guides report about $15,000–$35,000 for a conventional replacement, more for nitrogen-reducing or mounded systems.

Is there help paying for a septic repair?

Yes. The state septic tax credit is 60% of eligible costs up to $30,000 (max $4,000 a year, $18,000 total), and MassHousing and many towns offer low- or no-interest septic loans.
